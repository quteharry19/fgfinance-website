Premium Funding Code of Practice

Effective Date: September 2024 | Version: COP-NZ-2024-V1

Kiwi business owners and consumers seeking insurance premium funding deserve fair, transparent pricing. To support this, **FG Finance Limited** voluntarily operates under the standards of the **Insurance Premium Funding Code of Practice** (the "Code").

The Code demands that premium funding credit providers supply transparent, clear, and easily understandable pricing information. This empowers borrowers to compare credit options easily and make informed financial decisions.

Our Pricing Disclosures

Under the Code, we display two separate interest rates on all FG Finance loan documentation: the **Flat Rate** and the **Annual Percentage Rate (APR)**. Providing both metrics ensures complete clarity in how your loan interest costs are calculated.

1. What is the Flat Rate?

The Flat Rate is calculated as the **total credit charges divided by the original premium amount being funded**. It is a simple interest calculation that does not take into account the loan duration or the fact that you are gradually paying off the principal amount.

Example: Similar to the simple interest calculation on an interest-only facility where the principal remains unpaid until maturity. Note that premium funding loans are not interest-only facilities.

2. What is the Annual Percentage Rate (APR)?

The APR calculates the cost of the loan taking into account the **reducing principal balance over the term**, expressed as a standardized annual percentage rate. For both calculations, the rates are exclusive of administrative transaction fees.

Example: Standard practice for home loans or commercial bank loans where you pay regular principal and interest instalments, gradually reducing the outstanding debt balance over time.

Why is the APR higher than the Flat Rate?

Am I being charged more?

**No. The actual credit charges you pay in dollars are exactly the same under both rate disclosures.**

The APR is expressed as a higher percentage because it reflects interest charged on a **reducing principal balance**. As you pay your monthly instalments, the outstanding loan balance drops. Expressing the same credit charges against a shrinking balance results in a higher mathematical rate. Disclosing the APR is a finance industry standard that allows you to easily compare a premium funding loan against credit cards, business overdrafts, or short-term business credit lines.

Where is the APR shown?

The Flat Rate and APR are prominently displayed on all online loan acceptance screens in our broker portals, and are explicitly outlined inside the main credit schedule details of your **Offer to Borrow for Insurance Premiums**.
